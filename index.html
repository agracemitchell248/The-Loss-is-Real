Key Concept

Ambiguous Loss

Unlike a death, there's no funeral. When someone dies, society has rituals. When you lose your job, you're expected to update your LinkedIn within weeks.

What Makes Job Loss "Ambiguous"?

  • What's lost is still present: your skills, knowledge, and identity still exist, with nowhere to go
  • No clear timeline for resolution, no ceremonial closure
  • Unclear what you're grieving: the job? The routine? Your identity?
  • Others minimize it: "Something better will come along."

You're not just grieving a job. You're grieving your purpose, identity, the future you imagined, and often your sense of self-worth.

You have permission to grieve this fully. It's not dramatic. It's honest acknowledgment of a real loss.

Key Concept

Three Phases of Traumatic Displacement

Job loss isn't a single moment. It's a journey through distinct phases.

🎳 Phase 1: Leadup to the Loss

Feels like: Rumors, fear, watching others go, waiting for your turn.

Know: This anticipatory anxiety is real trauma. The waiting IS the trauma.

⚡ Phase 2: During the Loss

Feels like: Shock, grief, panic, sudden identity crisis.

Know: However you responded, crying, shutting down, autopilot, was your body keeping you safe.

🌊 Phase 3: Post-Loss

Feels like: Reactive decisions from fear, urgency about money, difficulty trusting, even in new jobs.

Know: Landing a new job doesn't automatically heal the trauma.

These phases aren't linear. You might be in more than one at once.
